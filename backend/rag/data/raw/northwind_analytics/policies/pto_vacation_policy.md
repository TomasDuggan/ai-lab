# PTO / Vacation Policy

## Overview
This document covers paid time off (PTO) accrual, usage, carryover, and
payout rules for all full-time employees at Northwind Analytics.

## Rules

- Full-time employees accrue PTO based on tenure:
  - 0–2 years: 15 days per year
  - 2–5 years: 20 days per year
  - 5+ years: 25 days per year
- PTO accrues monthly (e.g. an employee with 15 days/year accrues 1.25
  days per month), not in a lump sum at the start of the year.
- Part-time employees (working 20–29 hours/week) accrue PTO on a
  pro-rated basis, proportional to hours worked relative to a 40-hour
  work week.
- Employees must submit PTO requests through the HR portal at least
  5 business days in advance for absences of 3 or more consecutive days.
- Requests for 1–2 days off require at least 48 hours advance notice.
- Manager approval is required for all PTO requests. Managers must
  respond within 3 business days; if no response is given, the request
  is automatically approved.
- PTO cannot be taken during a company-wide blackout period (the last
  two weeks of each fiscal quarter), except for emergencies approved by
  HR directly.

## Carryover and payout

- Up to 5 unused PTO days may be carried over into the following
  calendar year. Any unused days beyond that cap are forfeited on
  December 31st, unless local labor law requires otherwise.
- Employees in jurisdictions where forfeiture is not legally permitted
  will instead receive a cash payout for unused days beyond the
  5-day carryover cap, calculated at their current daily pay rate.
- Upon resignation or termination, employees are paid out for all
  accrued, unused PTO, regardless of the reason for separation.

## Edge cases / exceptions

- Employees on an approved leave of absence (medical, parental, etc.)
  continue to accrue PTO during the first 90 days of leave only. Accrual
  pauses after 90 days and resumes upon return to active work.
- New hires may not use PTO during their first 30 days of employment,
  even if they have accrued a partial day, except in the case of a
  documented medical emergency.
- If a public holiday falls within an approved PTO period, that day is
  not deducted from the employee's PTO balance.
- Employees who transfer between full-time and part-time status mid-year
  have their accrual rate recalculated prospectively from the date of
  the change; past accrual is not retroactively adjusted.
